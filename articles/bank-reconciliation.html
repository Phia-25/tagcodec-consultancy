Bank Reconciliation: A Simple Guide

Bank reconciliation is the process of comparing the balance in an organization's accounting records with the balance shown by the bank statement and explaining the differences.

Why is it important?

Regular reconciliation helps identify timing differences, recording errors, omitted transactions, bank charges, and other items that need attention before financial statements are finalized.

Common reconciling items

Deposit in Transit

A deposit in transit is a deposit already recorded in the books but not yet reflected in the bank statement, often because the bank processes it on a later date.

Outstanding Check

An outstanding check is a check already recorded in the books but not yet presented to or cleared by the bank.

Simple process

  1. Obtain the bank statement and the relevant cash-in-bank ledger.
  2. Compare deposits, checks, withdrawals, and other transactions.
  3. Identify timing differences and recording errors.
  4. Prepare the reconciliation and necessary adjusting entries.
  5. Investigate unusual or unsupported differences.
Tip: A difference does not automatically mean that the books are wrong. First determine whether it is a normal timing difference or an actual recording issue.
